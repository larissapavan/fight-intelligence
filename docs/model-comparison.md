## How much does using today's career totals inflate a model?

The same gradient boosting as the candidate, on the same split, trained twice. Today's career totals include the fight being predicted and every later one, so a model that sees them is partly reading the answer.

| Model | Accuracy | ROC AUC | Log loss | Brier | Calibration error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| without today's totals (honest) | 62.3% | 0.666 | 0.651 | 0.230 | 0.025 |
| with today's career totals added (leaky) | 73.0% | 0.808 | 0.551 | 0.185 | 0.073 |

## What this does and does not show

- The candidate's numbers are honest: every feature is from before the fight, the test fights are all later than the training fights, and the model settings and calibration were chosen without looking at the test block.
- The deployed model's own training data is not available, so it is not known whether its reported accuracy came from this kind of leakage. The table above shows that a model fed today's career totals reaches that accuracy, and that the deployed model does not reach it on these fights.
- Striking and takedown history comes from ESPN's per-fight statistics (`data/raw/fight_stats.csv`), which cover 98.8% of the fights; a fighter's first fights have no history yet, so those rows have empty striking features.
- One test block, one split: read differences of a point or two as noise.
